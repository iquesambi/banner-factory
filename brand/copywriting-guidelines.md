# Copywriting guidelines

A short overview of how Elkjøp / Elgiganten sounds, to orient whoever (or
whatever) writes the banner copy. It is a direction, not a rulebook: the brand
guide (`Elkjop_Design_Guide_V1_9.4.pdf`, "Verbal identity", pp. 12–15) is the
source, and the real banners in `../examples/` show the voice in use.

## Who is speaking

Elkjøp is **the trusted advisor**: an honest friend who listens more than they
talk and always has a good answer. The brand believes in matchmaking, finding
the right product for the right person so they get more out of life with
technology.

Write like a store employee who likes the customer, not like a billboard
shouting at them.

## Tone of voice

| Trait | In practice |
|---|---|
| **For everyone** | Friendly, relaxed and down to earth. Never pushy or demanding, never fancy or pretentious. |
| **With a smile** | Warm, light humour is welcome when it shows insight into real life. No sarcasm, no slapstick, no jokes at anyone's expense. |
| **Be inspiring** | We love what we do, and it should show. |
| **Keep it simple** | Tech is complicated; our job is to make it easy to understand. Plain words over jargon. |

## What good banner copy does

Lessons from our copy team's reviews of generated banners:

- **The headline names the product or its biggest benefit.** A headline that
  could sit on any product in the category reads as generic and machine-made.
  A concrete, true fact about the product beats a vague promise.
- **It is about the actual product.** Ink is not paper; a LEGO set is not the
  video game it is themed on. Make sure the reader knows what is being sold.
- **Each line has its own job.** The headline carries the main idea, the
  supporting line adds a second one, and a description says plainly what the
  product is. Lines should not repeat each other.
- **Benefits over specifications.** Say what the product does for the person.
  Raw measurements, publisher names and features every competitor has are not
  interesting.
- **Less is fine.** If there is only one thing worth saying, say one thing.
- **Short and spoken.** Headlines are a few words; the guide suggests 30–45
  characters and never more than 60. Headlines take no closing punctuation,
  except a question mark, since the brand likes to ask questions.

## What to avoid

- Manufactured urgency and shouting ("Hurry!", "Last chance!!").
- Empty superlatives and hype.
- Technical jargon the customer has to decode.
- Any claim that is not supported by the product data: prices, savings,
  delivery, warranty, stock or features must never be invented.

## Language

The product feed is Danish (Elgiganten DK), but the banner is written in the
language chosen for it. Copy should read as natural, native language, not as a
translation of the feed text.
